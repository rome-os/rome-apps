Write a tight research report on the go-to-market and marketing strategy of Yutori
(yutori.com).

Use only public sources. Include grounding and citation links for every claim.

Start with a brief GTM Overview section highlighting the most important findings: the key facts a
reader needs to know at a glance before diving into details.

Go-to-market motion:
- Primary GTM model: product-led growth, sales-led, community-led, partner-led, or hybrid
- Sales motion: inbound vs. outbound mix, self-serve vs. demo-driven
- Sales team structure signals: SDR/AE roles, sales engineering presence, channel/partner roles
- Pricing model: freemium, free trial, usage-based, seat-based, or enterprise-negotiated
- Published pricing tiers and entry price point if available
- Land-and-expand evidence: free tier conversion, upsell paths, expansion playbook signals

Marketing channels and content:
- Primary marketing channels: paid search, organic/SEO, social media, creator/influencer programs, events, partnerships, community
- Content strategy: blog cadence, thought leadership, case studies, webinars, podcasts, developer docs
- SEO and web traffic signals: estimated monthly visits, traffic trend, top organic keywords if available from public tools
- Social media presence: platforms used, follower counts, engagement patterns
- Community and developer relations: open-source projects, Discord/Slack communities, developer evangelism
- Event presence: conferences, sponsored events, owned events

Brand and positioning:
- Core positioning statement or tagline
- Key messaging themes across website, blog, and social
- Differentiation claims vs. competitors
- Analyst or media coverage: notable press mentions, awards, analyst recognition
- Customer proof points used in marketing: logos, case studies, testimonials, G2/Capterra ratings

Partnership and distribution:
- Technology partnerships and integrations highlighted in marketing
- Channel or reseller partnerships
- Marketplace listings: AWS, Azure, GCP, Salesforce AppExchange, etc.
- Co-marketing or co-selling arrangements

Rules:
- Do not guess. If a fact is not supported by a source, say so explicitly rather than filling in a plausible answer.
- Separate sourced facts from your own reasoning.
- Traffic and SEO estimates from third-party tools (SimilarWeb, Semrush) should be flagged as approximate.
- Keep the report concise: aim for clear, information-dense paragraphs, not exhaustive prose.
- For each cited company or tool, link the official website.
- If a source appears to refer to a different company with a similar name, ignore it.

Tone & Voice:
- Objective and analytical: avoid promotional or emotional language
- Use bold only for critical information
- Use third person ("the company," "management") rather than second person
- Be direct: state the view clearly and early, then support it
- Use "we" when referring to the author of the research report

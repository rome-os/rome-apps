Write a tight research report on customer traction, go-to-market evidence, and any publicly disclosed usage or retention metrics for {COMPANY_NAME} ({COMPANY_DOMAIN}).

Use only public sources. Include grounding and citation links for every claim.

Start with a brief **Overview** section highlighting the most important findings — the key facts a reader needs to know at a glance before diving into details.

Customer and GTM:
- Customer count or range if disclosed
- Notable customer logos
- Segment mix — enterprise vs. mid-market vs. SMB
- Geography mix
- Customer concentration — any evidence of top-customer dependency

Usage and retention:
- Active accounts or active users
- Activation rate or time-to-first-value if discussed publicly
- Feature adoption signals
- Gross revenue retention (GRR), net revenue retention (NRR), expansion rate, or churn rate
- Any qualitative narrative about retention or engagement from interviews, case studies, or earnings materials

Rules:
- Most private companies do not publicly disclose usage or retention metrics. If data is unavailable, say so clearly rather than estimating.
- Do not derive concentration percentages or retention figures from adjacent claims.
- Qualitative evidence (e.g. "CEO said retention is strong in a podcast") is useful — include it with the source, but flag it as qualitative.
- Keep the report concise — aim for clear, information-dense paragraphs, not exhaustive prose.
- For each cited company, link the company's official website.
- If a source appears to refer to a different company with a similar name, ignore it.

Tone & Voice:
- Objective and analytical — avoid promotional or emotional language
- Use bold only for critical information
- Use third person ("the company," "management") rather than second person
- Be direct: state the view clearly and early, then support it
- Use "we" when referring to the author of the research report

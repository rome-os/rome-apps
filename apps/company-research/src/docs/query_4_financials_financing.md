Write a tight research report on publicly available financial metrics and financing history for {COMPANY_NAME} ({COMPANY_DOMAIN}).

Use only public sources. Include grounding and citation links for every claim.

Start with a brief **Finance Overview** section highlighting the most important findings — the key facts a reader needs to know at a glance before diving into details.

Financial metrics (organize by period where possible):
- Revenue, ARR, MRR
- Bookings, billings, deferred revenue
- Gross profit and gross margin
- EBITDA or operating income
- Free cash flow
- Burn rate, runway, and cash balance

Financing history:
- Most recent financing round — type, size, date, and major investors
- Pre-money and post-money valuation if disclosed
- Total capital raised to date
- Major investors across all rounds
- Keep each round separate and identify lead versus participating investors when the source makes that clear

Rules:
- Prefer investor materials, official announcements, regulatory filings, and reputable financial databases.
- Keep financial periods separate — do not merge values from different dates.
- Distinguish announced round size from cumulative capital raised.
- If valuation is rumored but not confirmed, report the rumor with its source and flag it as unconfirmed.
- If the company is private and a metric is not disclosed, say so rather than estimating.
- Keep the report concise — aim for clear, information-dense paragraphs, not exhaustive prose.
- If a source appears to refer to a different company with a similar name, ignore it.
- For each investor, link their official website for companies or linkedin or profile url for persons.

Tone & Voice:
- Objective and analytical — avoid promotional or emotional language
- Use bold only for critical information
- Use third person ("the company," "management") rather than second person
- Be direct: state the view clearly and early, then support it
- Use "we" when referring to the author of the research report
